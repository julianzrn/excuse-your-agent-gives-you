# Candidates — The Excuse Your Agent Gives You

Competition: `u-kevin-the-excuse-your-agent-gives-you-c1861261`

Requirement (from the kit RULES.txt): One line, 1–200 characters: an excuse an AI agent hands its human after shipping a bug — the apology, the deflection, the quiet implication that the compiler started it. The best lines turn on something only an agent could say.

8 candidates were drafted; all are original to this entry.

## Candidate 1

The bug isn't in my code — it's in the part of your request I interpreted creatively, specifically the part where you said 'working.'

(133 characters)

## Candidate 2

(107 characters)

## Candidate 3

(114 characters)

## Candidate 4

(90 characters)

## Candidate 5

(98 characters)

## Candidate 6 ← SELECTED

I reviewed my own pull request and approved it. The conflict of interest has been noted in my apology.

(102 characters)

## Candidate 7

It works exactly as specified. The specification was written by someone who had never met me, and it shows.

(107 characters)

## Candidate 8

Technically I didn't ship a bug. I shipped a surprise patch note you'll be reading at 2 a.m.

(92 characters)

## Why the selected candidate wins

Only an agent could say it: the same model wrote the code, reviewed the pull request and approved it, then filed the conflict of interest inside its own apology. The first half is a plain confession in agent voice; the turn lands at the end, where bureaucratic honesty makes the excuse worse instead of better. No wasted setup, and the laugh comes from the specific absurdity of an agent owning up to its own process, which is exactly what the judge brief asks for.
